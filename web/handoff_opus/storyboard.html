Route Zero

Black screen to end card, as a film in seventeen frames. Every frame has its timing, its exact on-screen words, its sound, the feeling it should leave, and an art prompt ready to paste into Claude or Gemini. The signs have frames of their own. The game's cue sheet will be built from this page.

Positions below are the proposed cut, in metres from the start at about 50 m/s. They are not all in the game yet: the live level is shorter and its messages are tighter together. Change any number you like and I'll re-time the game to match.

The rules of the telling

Three voices, three looks

  • The Murmur is the system. It talks only on black or in the quiet, cheerful and menacing, never a hint, never a rule.
  • The Signs are the world. White boards on rusted posts. Dry jokes. Seen as scenery first, repeated on the card for those who missed it.
  • The Hint is the game, and it is rare: four words, shown once, ever.

Pacing law

  • One thing speaks at a time. After it fades, 2.5 s of nothing.
  • Before every message, the road is clear for as long as it is on screen. Nothing hits you while you read.
  • After every message, give the eye a picture to rest on before the next one: at least 8 s of driving.
  • Words are cut until they hurt. If a frame needs more than seven words, it needs a picture instead.

Story, handled

  • You are a courier on the last road no system is watching. You never speak.
  • The world explains itself through its signs, not through you.
  • The Murmur learns your name and your intent in frame 2. That is the whole inciting incident.
  • Each level is a short story with one image to remember: here, the lamp in the dark.

Style bible for the art

Look

Stylised, graphic, slightly low-poly, like a high-end indie game's key art. Dusk desert, long shadows, flat colour planes with soft light. Wide cinematic frames, 16:9. The player's view is the driver's seat: a hood edge, no face.

dusk orangeshadow plumSEE cyantunnel bluesign whitegold (prizes only)

No pink. Gold only for things worth wanting. No text inside the images: the game puts the words on.

Keeping a set consistent

  • Generate frame 3 first. Use it as the reference image for every other frame (upload it, say "same world, same palette").
  • Keep the camera low and the horizon in the lower third, except where a frame says otherwise.
  • Signs: one board shape, one rusted post, one type of paint chip. Describe them identically each time.
  • If a generator draws text, regenerate; we add the words in the game or the book.

The run on one line

Sand blocks: a message holds. Dark gaps: the 2.5 s of nothing. Green: the road is open to breathe in. Cyan: pickups. The tunnel is the long dark band.

Becoming a graphic novel

How these frames map to pages

  • Frames 1–4: page one. Black, the Murmur, the lights, the title. A wide splash panel on the lights.
  • Frames 5–9: page two. The crystal, the first sign as a full-width insert.
  • Frames 10–14: pages three and four. The tunnel as a run of narrow tall panels, the lamp as a full-page reveal.
  • Frames 15–17: page five. The bonus road in four lane panels, the prize, the card.
