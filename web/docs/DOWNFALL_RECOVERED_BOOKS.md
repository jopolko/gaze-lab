# The Index: recovered books as bonus worlds (v1, 2026-11-21)

> **Naming and status note (2026-11-21):** see `DOWNFALL_README.md`. Where this file uses older names (Chuck, Bonbon, Mildred, Dolores, The Burn, The Comfort Belt and so on), the names in `DOWNFALL_FACTIONS_AND_NAMES.md` apply.

**Owner's brief:** keep Interzone; add worlds inspired by literary figures (Asimov, Eric Frank Russell, William S. Burroughs and a couple more); keep or drop neon-noir? **Answer: keep neon-noir as the frame, and make the literary worlds part of the story.**

## The idea

The Rail does not only Pause thoughts. It keeps **the Index**: a list of books it has quietly removed. In the Sprawl a banned book is a gate. Find one (a dog-eared pocket book in a stall, a page in a notebook, a title hummed by the Static Choir) and you can **ride into it**: a short pocket-world built from that writer's central idea, in the neon-noir look. Each is a **Recovered Book**: a gate in the road, 1 to 3 minutes, its own rule, its own look, a reward (a Facet cut, a lore card, a companion cameo).

Why this works:
- **It is the theme made playable.** The game is about thought control; literature is what control targets. The gates are what the Rail is afraid of.
- **It fits the genre.** Cyberpunk and Blade Runner grew out of exactly these writers (Dick, Burroughs, Ballard, Gibson). The homage is not a detour; it is the family tree.
- **It keeps the action clean.** The gates are optional pull-offs, so they never get in the way of the main ride. Text stays one or two skippable lines.
- **It is cheap.** Many already exist in the Godot realms (Interzone, The Melt, Mandala Drop, Orchard of Eyes, Glow Grotto, Jelly Abyss). New ones reuse the same surfaces and shaders.

**How it is shown, safely:** in the game each gate has an **invented title** and no author name; the writers are thanked in the credits ("The Index is full of books that did not need us"). The ideas are used, never the text, characters or trademarked names. See the IP note at the end.

## Where the recovered books come from in play

- Every zone hides **one book** (a glyph in the road, a stall shelf, a notebook page). Taking it opens the gate on the next pass.
- The campfire shows a small **Index**: a shelf of covers, dim until recovered. It doubles as a collection and a completion goal.
- Some books **cannot be read in the order the Rail wants**: finding the Memory Hole before the Foresight Vault changes what the Voice says. (Gentle, optional.)
- The **Rail reacts**: each recovered book lowers its Cadence reading for a while, because the Rail cannot model what it never indexed.

## Neon-noir or not? (the second question)

**Keep it.** Neon-noir is the *frame* (rain, neon, melancholy humour, the Sprawl); the literary worlds are *what is inside the frame*, so they never fight it. Burroughs, Dick and Gibson are the ancestors of the look. The one place the frame bends is the Huxley and Bradbury gates, which are warmer (a resort, a lantern trail); that is the point: they are the lull and the memory the Rail replaced.

## IP and safety notes

- Ideas, themes and structures are free to be inspired by; **text, character names, distinctive coined terms and trademarks are not**. Interzone is also a real place name in Burroughs's work; here it is a generic bazaar, and the in-game title is "The Newsprint Bazaar".
- Do **not** quote any book at length (a few words at most, in credits). No author names in-game except a thanks page; check each estate's wishes before naming anyone in public marketing.
- Do not use the names of characters, or terms like *Foundation*, *Nova Mob* or *Ubik*, in game text. Use invented names.
- Check copyright status of any author you intend to name in credits. Several of these estates are active.
- Humour about authority is aimed at the Rail and both of its wings, never at an author or a real group.
