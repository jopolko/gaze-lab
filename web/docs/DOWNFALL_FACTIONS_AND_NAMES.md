# Factions, names and flavour: the neon-noir pass (v1, 2026-11-21)

**Owner's direction:** more cyberpunk / Blade Runner, not Borderlands. **From Borderlands keep only:** the driving between locales, the humour, the atmospheric and characterful touch. **Keep:** the characters (their roles and jokes were liked). **Want:** cool factions and trippy names; the old names (Chuck, Bonbon, Mildred, Mama Quack, Clive, Barry the Balloon, Dave the Waddler) read as Borderlands-silly and go.

Inspiration only; nothing here copies a protected name. I avoided Blade Runner's own terms (replicant, Tyrell, Voight-Kampff, baseline, off-world) and built parallel ideas in our own words. All names are working names and need a trademark and domain check before public use.

---

## 0. The look and the mood (the atmosphere, in one paragraph)

Rain-slick neon over eggshell concrete. Holo-ads in a soothing voice. Steam from noodle stalls under flyovers. Dead phones, cooling towers, a sun that is a tuned colour temperature. Everything the Rail approves is **pale, pastel, brushed-chrome and kind**; everything it cannot read is **saturated, glitching, alive**. Synth washes and slow arpeggios under a hard kick when you fall (original music only). Dry, melancholy humour in the narration, never a wink. The sprawl is a **continent of themed zones**, so each biome is a district: the same world, seven moods.

**Reconciling cyberpunk with the biomes:** every biome is a **zone of the Sprawl**, because the Rail themed its districts. Cold Storage is a cryo-archive on a glacier; the Comfort Belt is a pleasure-retail strip; the Burn is a reclamation flat and re-education campus; the Underlay is the transit and conduit city; the Mirror is the server district; the Broadcast is the entertainment boulevard; the Commons is the one patch of land off every grid. Season 2 is the drowned coast. That is why a glacier, a candy strip and a desert can share one story.

## 5. Taking what helps from the other design (the "Frequency Courier" blueprint)

You asked whether a pasted blueprint (a single courier whose vehicle changes with each biome) can help. **Yes, in five places; no, in three.**

**Take:**
1. **One machine that changes shape with the zone, with an in-fiction reason.** Our rig changes per biome already; this gives it a reason and a spectacle. The reason in our world: the Descender's machine is a **Loom**, a salvage chassis running adaptive firmware that re-forms itself to match whatever the Rail's local "band" can't classify (ice, sand, water, air). Crossing a zone border is a short morph: the wireframe melts into the next rig (the Voronoi-shatter transition we already planned, one cheap effect on the existing picture pass).
2. **A consistent control map across rigs.** Head **yaw** steers everywhere. That is already our rule, and the blueprint's table is a good checklist for what each rig does with it (carve, trim a sail, bank a wing, lean on a rail).
3. **Mode-specific style actions as scoring.** *Barrel time* (surf), *vector lock* (keeping with a wind ribbon in a sail-sled), *terminal thread* (diving through rings and flaring late), *apex drift* (a held drift). They map neatly onto Season 2's water and air rigs and onto our existing Chains / Clean / Speed stars.
4. **Carry the multiplier through the border.** Crossing a zone without braking keeps your flow multiplier into the next rig (a medal for a "zero-brake" run). Our journey already links the biomes; this makes the link rewarding.
5. **Two new zones.** A **sail-sled on a mirrored salt flat** (slow, wide, wind ribbons you can see, very kind to head steering) joins Season 2 as **THE SALT MIRROR**. And an **abyssal sonar run** (pitch-black trench, a ping you aim, glowing ASCII-style walls) is a strong fit for the existing **Jelly Abyss** gate, extended into a full level. Both use surfaces and the echo power (Chime Spore).

**Skip or change:**
1. **Pitch (looking up and down) as a second control.** Risky on a phone: tilting the phone changes the apparent pitch, and fine pitch is a weak spot of the tracker. The Godot source already has a better optional second input: **expressions** (raise eyebrows to hop, open wide to eat, squint to brace; RunRig 4296, 7554, 10665). I'd offer a hop on a generous eyebrow raise, off by default, tested before any of it is promised.
2. **"Gaze-lock" scoring** (look at a hazard until the last moment). We steer by head turn, so looking at something steers you into it. Keep the **near-miss graze** as a bonus (passing close without a stumble) and drop the gaze part.
3. **Screen-space Ben-Day dots and a story about "waveform energy".** Dots go on surfaces (the foam, the cloud sprites), per our rule; and our story already has its own spine, the Rail and the Fall Line. The courier hook is a good tone note, not a replacement.

**Its two questions, answered:** *Input* is a phone webcam head-tracker (on device, face and head only). *Structure* is linear episodes now (the journey), with an open-world proof still pending.
